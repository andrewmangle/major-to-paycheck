# Major to Paycheck: notes for Claude Code

## What this is
A one-page prototype for Bowie State University College of Business students, built by Dr. Andrew Mangle
(owner). A student picks a program (10 undergraduate programs) and a place to live (Baltimore, Washington DC,
Northern Virginia) and sees, for each job the program leads to: early-career pay, monthly take-home after
2026 taxes, one-bedroom rent, what is left, a "living alone" verdict, how many people hold that job locally,
what employers screen for, how to close the gap, and links to openings and a sign-up form.

## Why it exists
Student customer-discovery interviews (Andrew's class) showed students are not short on job listings.
They are stuck on: what jobs their major leads to, what those pay, whether they can afford to live on their
own, and how to get hired without experience. This replaced the earlier "Fed Intern Drop" listings-first
approach. Fed Intern Drop (@fedinterndrop) and the Mangle Metric (andrewmangle.com) are the next steps on the page.

## Files
- `data.py`: all content. Programs, roles (title, BLS SOC code, what you do, what employers screen for,
  how to close the gap, notes, first-job route), places (rent, tax jurisdiction), 2026 tax tables,
  GS pay, sign-up form settings, sources.
- `bls_wages.json`: official BLS OEWS May 2025 25th percentile, median and employment for every SOC code
  used, for the Baltimore (BAL, area 12580) and DC (DCM, area 47900) metros. Generated by load_bls.py.
- `load_bls.py`: `python3 load_bls.py MSA_M2025_dl.xlsx` (from oesm25ma.zip at
  https://www.bls.gov/oes/special-requests/oesm25ma.zip). Re-run each spring when BLS publishes new data,
  then update the file name and "May 2025" wording.
- `build.py`: computes pay, taxes, take-home, verdicts and links, injects JSON into template.html.
  Writes `index.html` (complete page, served by GitHub Pages from the repo root) and
  `major-to-paycheck.html` (fragment for the Claude artifact host, not committed).
  Run `python3 build.py` after any change.
- `template.html`: the page (HTML, CSS tokens for light and dark themes, vanilla JS). No build tools.
- `form/setup_signup_form.gs`: Apps Script that creates the sign-up form questions and prints the
  pre-fill entry numbers. Its PROGRAMS and PLACES lists must match data.py (tests check this).
- `tests/`: `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests`. Run before every push.

## How the numbers work
- Early-career pay = BLS 25th percentile for the job in the metro.
- Exceptions: Economist uses 2026 GS-7 step 1, Washington-Baltimore locality ($57,736).
  Cybersecurity analyst and database administrator budget on IT support pay (SOC 15-1232);
  data scientist budgets on market research analyst pay (13-1161). These use `first_job` in data.py.
- DC and Northern Virginia share the DC metro wage data and HUD rent area; they differ only by taxes.
- Taxes: single filer, standard deduction, no retirement contributions. Federal 2026 brackets;
  Maryland state plus Baltimore City local 3.2%; DC; Virginia. FICA 7.65%. Verified by hand at $65,000.
- Verdict: rent at or under 30% of gross monthly pay = comfortable, 30 to 40% = tight, over 40% = hard.
- Rent: HUD FY2027 one-bedroom Fair Market Rent (Baltimore $1,625, Washington area $2,204).

## Sign-up form
`SIGNUP` in data.py points to Andrew's Google Form. Card buttons pre-fill program, job and place.
Running `form/setup_signup_form.gs` prints the form's published URL and each question's `entry.NNNN`
number; put the URL in `SIGNUP["form"]` and the numbers in `SIGNUP["entries"]`, then rebuild.
(Manual route: in Google Forms use "Get pre-filled link", fill those three questions, copy the link.)
Pre-filled values must match the form's choices exactly: program names match the Program dropdown, and
`SIGNUP["place_choices"]` maps each place to its checkbox label ("Washington DC", not "Washington, DC").
